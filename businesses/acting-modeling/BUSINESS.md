## What this is

Austin's own career as talent — **modeling-led, with acting as the second track.** Unlike every
other venture in this workspace, the product here is Austin himself: his look, his reel, his
reliability on set, and his reputation with the small number of people in this region who book
talent.

That changes how the agents work. There is no listing to publish and no store to optimize. The
work is: build the assets that let him be submitted, find and vet real opportunities, get him in
front of legitimate representation, and keep him bookable.

## Why Wilmington is a genuine advantage (not wishful thinking)

Wilmington is one of the very few US cities outside LA / NYC / Atlanta with a **standing studio
facility**. EUE/Screen Gems Studios was sold to Cinespace in 2023 and still operates here as part
of a network of 109 active stages across Wilmington, Atlanta, Chicago, Toronto, and Studio
Babelsberg. Productions come here, cast locally for background and small speaking roles, and hire
local print/commercial talent for regional campaigns.

The practical meaning: Austin does **not** have to move to work. Most people trying to break in
are competing in markets with 100× the talent supply. He is already standing in a working market
with a shallow local talent pool. That is the single biggest structural asset this venture has.

Caveat, honestly: the market is real but not booming — post-strike Wilmington has seen slower
stretches, and volume swings with what happens to be shooting. Treat it as a steady trickle to be
worked consistently, not a gold rush.

### The income math, stated plainly

Non-union background work in North Carolina pays roughly **$100–200 for a 10–12 hour day**. NC is
a right-to-work state, so there is no union floor forcing rates up; budgets set them. Regional
print/commercial modeling pays substantially better per day than background work, but books far
less often, especially before representation.

So: replacing a full income *from day rates alone* would take a volume of bookings a brand-new,
unrepresented, non-union talent will not get in 90 days. The path that actually gets there runs
through representation (an agent brings volume and better-paying clients), a real reel and
tearsheet book, and eventually upgrading toward union work. Every 90-day action in this venture is
chosen because it moves that longer clock forward — not because a day rate this month is the point.

Nobody in this workspace will pretend otherwise to make the dashboard look better.

## Hard constraints (these are not negotiable)

1. **Austin never pays an agency to represent him.** Legitimate agencies make money by taking a
   commission (commonly ~10–20%) on work they book. Signing fees, registration fees, "website
   placement" fees, mandatory in-house photographer packages, and paid "training" as a condition of
   representation are the defining marker of a scam. No exceptions, no "but this one seems real."
2. **Every casting notice and every agency gets vetted before Austin responds.** See the vetting
   checklist in the `talent-ops` skill. Fake casting calls that harvest personal data, photos, and
   money are endemic in this industry and specifically target new talent.
3. **No agent in this workspace submits Austin to anything, contacts an agency, or replies to a
   casting director on his behalf.** Everything is drafted into `submissions/` or `inbox/` for him
   to send himself. This is the standard HQ draft-and-recommend rule and it matters more here,
   because these are real people in a small local industry and his name is on it permanently.
4. **Never send measurements, personal photos, address, or ID to an unvetted contact.** A real
   agency asks for digitals and stats *after* you have verified who they are — not in a cold DM.
5. **No fabricated credits, ever.** A padded resume is discovered immediately in a market this
   small and ends a career before it starts. School theatre is listed as school theatre.

## Platform notes (researched 2026-07-28 — spend nothing until asset stack exists)

- **Casting Networks** — the priority for commercial and modeling work, and the platform most
  regional agencies actually work through. Plans from ~$19.99/mo or ~$149.99/yr.
- **Actors Access** — cheapest serious option (~$68/yr), strongest for film/TV submissions.
- **Backstage** — ~$199.95/yr, highest listing volume and the most beginner-friendly, but a lot of
  its listings are unpaid or low-paid. Reasonable *only* while building credits.
- **Free tier first**: Project Casting and free platforms open background work with no
  subscription. Local Wilmington background casting companies post directly to Facebook groups and
  their own sites — free and higher-yield than any subscription for extras work specifically.

**Recommendation**: create the free profiles and work local background casting first. Buy exactly
one paid platform once the headshots exist, and make it Casting Networks given the modeling-led
focus. Do not subscribe to three at once.

## Honest risks

1. **Scams are the #1 threat to a new model.** More new talent loses money to fake agencies than
   ever loses a booking to competition. Constraints 1, 2, and 4 above exist for this reason.
2. **Modeling has physical gating that acting doesn't.** High-fashion and runway have narrow
   height/measurement requirements; commercial, print, promotional, e-comm, and lifestyle work are
   far more open and are where the money actually is regionally. Once Austin's stats are on file,
   `talent-agent` will scope the realistic lane honestly instead of aiming him at runway by default.
3. **Rejection volume is the job.** Booking rates in the low single-digit percent are normal, not a
   signal something is wrong. The metric that matters early is *submissions sent*, not offers.
4. **It's slow before it's fast.** Representation, then volume, then rate increases, then union.
   Skipping steps is how people get scammed.
5. **This is a real time commitment on set days** — a background day is 10–12 hours and unglamorous.
   Worth knowing before saying yes to the first one.
