## Pillar 1 — POD Lifestyle/Humor Apparel (via Printify/Printful, no minimums)

Per Part 3's finding: existing disc-golf apparel makers (VII Apparel Co., Disc Store, MyLocker,
Sports Gear Swag) are overwhelmingly team/league jersey businesses. These three concepts are
deliberately casual/lifestyle/humor-forward instead — the confirmed open lane. Printify's
sublimated shirts are unisex-only (a real, cited platform limitation), so initial sizing should be
planned around that rather than assumed full gendered size-range parity from day one.

### SKU 1 — "Chains Hit Different" Unisex Tee

**Design concept** (for a designer or AI image tool to execute): centered chest graphic, retro
sunset/gradient circle behind a simple line-art disc golf basket silhouette, chains rendered as a
loose hand-drawn scribble catching the light. Arched text above the graphic reads "CHAINS HIT
DIFFERENT" in a bold, slightly worn/distressed varsity-style font — the "worn" texture reads as
lived-in merch rather than corporate-clean, matching the humor/lifestyle tone rather than a
team-jersey look. Single graphic, no sponsor-logo clutter. Recommend offering in 2-3 colors
(black, forest green, sand/khaki) since Printify's print-on-demand model makes color variants
essentially free to list.

**Product description draft**: "For anyone who's felt that specific kind of joy when a putt
rattles the chains instead of clanging off the pole. 'Chains Hit Different' is a soft, everyday
unisex tee for disc golfers who want something that doesn't scream 'tournament jersey' — just a
shirt that gets it. Pairs with literally anything you'd wear to the course or afterward."

**Price range**: $24–$28. *Verifiable*: standard Printify/Printful unisex tee blanks plus
print typically land retail prices in this band across the POD industry broadly — this is a
general POD-apparel pricing convention, not a disc-golf-specific figure, so treat as a solid
starting estimate rather than a guaranteed number until an actual Printify mockup/cost calculator
run is done.

### SKU 2 — "It's a Mando, Not a Drama" Unisex Hoodie

**Design concept**: small-scale front-chest print (not a giant back graphic, to keep per-unit
print cost down) — a simple cartoon disc mid-flight crossing a small triangular "mando" flag
icon, with "IT'S A MANDO, NOT A DRAMA" set in a clean sans-serif underneath in two lines. The joke
targets a real, specific disc golf frustration (mandatory-line rulings causing arguments) that any
regular player will immediately recognize — inside-joke humor aimed at the community itself, not
generic golf-pun territory. Single-color print (e.g., white ink on a heather-gray or black hoodie)
to keep the initial cost estimate conservative.

**Product description draft**: "Every group has had the argument. This hoodie is for the people
who've been on both sides of a mando ruling and lived to tell about it. Soft mid-weight fleece,
front-chest print, built for cold-morning rounds and everything after."

**Price range**: $42–$48. *Verifiable*: general POD hoodie pricing convention (fleece blanks cost
meaningfully more than tees industry-wide) — same caveat as SKU 1, a category-level estimate, not
a disc-golf-specific verified number.

### SKU 3 — "Flight Numbers Don't Lie" Retro Tee

**Design concept**: broader-appeal graphic (less inside-joke, more "looks cool even if you don't
play") styled like a vintage weather chart or vinyl-record label — a circular badge showing the
four real flight-number stats (Speed / Glide / Turn / Fade) as a simple retro data grid, muted
retro color palette (cream background, rust-orange and forest-green ink). This is the design meant
to work as everyday streetwear, not just course apparel, widening the buyer pool beyond active
players to friends/family/gift buyers.

**Product description draft**: "Speed, glide, turn, fade — the four numbers every disc golfer
argues about at the shop counter, laid out like a vintage weather chart. A retro-styled tee that
works as well off the course as on it."

**Price range**: $24–$28 (same tee-blank category as SKU 1).

## Pillar 2 — Accessories (sourced via MVP Disc Sports dropship or Disc Golf Deals USA wholesale)

Both suppliers were confirmed in `marketing/supplier-outreach-research.md` to carry these
categories (MVP's dropship program explicitly covers "Baskets, Bags, and Accessories"; Disc Golf
Deals USA has a dedicated Accessories collection). No specific supplier SKU/part number is claimed
here — real product identity would be confirmed once a dealer account is actually approved and the
dropship portlet/wholesale catalog is visible.

### SKU 4 — Quick-Dry Microfiber Disc Golf Towel

**What it is**: a standard clip-on microfiber towel — the accessory-comparison content pillar's
first natural subject ("best disc golf towel" is a real, searchable buying-guide topic). Multiple
suppliers (Gotta Go Gotta Throw, Disc Golf Deals USA, Dynamic Discs, Minnesota Preserve) confirmed
to carry a dedicated towels collection, so this category is real and sourceable — exact per-unit
wholesale cost wasn't retrievable without an active dealer login.

**Product description draft**: "A clip-on microfiber towel sized for actual disc golf use — quick
to dry a wet grip between throws, small enough to hang off a bag without getting in the way.
Nothing fancy, just the accessory every round needs and most bags don't have."

**Price range**: $12–$18 retail. *Flagged*: this range is estimated from general disc-golf-towel
market positioning (comparable clip-on microfiber towels at other disc golf retailers cluster in
this band); an exact wholesale cost from either target supplier was not retrievable in this pass.

### SKU 5 — Interlocking Mini Marker Disc

**What it is**: a small blank/stock marker disc, used to mark a lie on the ground per official
rules — a real, ruleset-relevant accessory (not a novelty item), which also gives the
content pillar a natural "do you actually need one of these?" rules-explainer angle. Confirmed
as a real product category carried by DGA, Innova, Gotta Go Gotta Throw, Infinite Discs, and
others.

**Product description draft**: "A regulation-legal mini marker disc for marking your lie — small,
pocket-sized, and something most new players don't realize they need until their first tournament
round. Simple, useful, and cheap enough to grab a couple of."

**Price range**: $3–$6. *Verified via price check*: search results for comparable mini marker
discs (DGA, Innova, Mint Discs, Gotta Go Gotta Throw) show a consistent $2.99–$5.99 cluster, with
budget options under $2 and premium/custom minis running higher — $3–$6 is a grounded estimate for
a standard (not custom-stamped) mini.

### SKU 6 — 16ft Telescoping Disc Retriever

**What it is**: a telescoping pole with a hook/wire head for retrieving discs from water hazards
or out-of-reach spots — a real, commonly stocked accessory category (Dynamic Discs, Gotta Go Gotta
Throw, and multiple Amazon/Walmart listings confirmed) and a good "worth it or not" comparison-
content subject given the price spread in this category.

**Product description draft**: "A 16-foot telescoping retriever for the discs that end up in
water, brush, or just out of arm's reach. Collapses down small enough to clip to a bag, extends
when you actually need it — the accessory nobody buys until the first disc they lose to a pond."

**Price range**: $25–$45. *Verified via price check*: comparable 16ft telescoping retrievers
(Rogue Iron at $49.99, Dynamic Discs' retriever, various Amazon/Walmart listings) cluster in the
$20–$50 range depending on build quality and head-attachment count; $25–$45 is a reasonable
starting estimate for a mid-tier version, not the premium end.

## Pillar 3 — Pre-Dyed Disc / Dye-Supply Corner (small, curated — not the core angle)

Per Part 3: actual disc dyeing is artisan/hands-on work Austin hasn't been shown to have skill in,
so this pillar is deliberately scoped as **dropshipping already-dyed discs and dye supplies**, not
becoming a dyer. Both item types were confirmed to exist as real, currently-sold products during
this draft's research pass.

### SKU 7 — Pre-Dyed Disc (single curated design, supplier: Disc Golf Deals USA's dyed-discs
collection)

**What it is**: one specific pre-dyed disc design, resold rather than dyed in-house — the "curated,
not full-catalog" version of the dye-corner idea. Disc Golf Deals USA confirmed to run a dedicated
Custom/Dyed Discs collection; exact design/plastic/mold would be chosen once dealer access is live
rather than invented here.

**Product description draft**: "A one-off, already-dyed disc — no two exactly alike, picked from a
small rotating selection rather than a full custom-order catalog. For players who want something
that looks different in the bag without ordering a custom dye job themselves."

**Price range**: $20–$35. *Verified via price check*: comparable pre-dyed discs across eBay,
Disc Golf Core, and other dye retailers span roughly $8–$60 depending on plastic/brand/complexity,
with a $20–$40 cluster for standard (non-premium-artist) pre-dyed discs — $20–$35 is a grounded
mid-range estimate for a curated, non-premium pick.

### SKU 8 — Beginner Disc Dye Starter Kit

**What it is**: a real, currently-sold product — Disc Golf Deals USA lists a "Beginner Disc Golf
Dye Kit" directly (confirmed via search, not invented), and PRO Chemical's starter dye set (sold on
Amazon and via SuperFly Disc Golf) is a comparable, independently confirmed product in the same
category. This is the one item in the catalog that could double as content-pillar material itself
("dye your own vs. buy pre-dyed" comparison, tying back to SKU 7).

**Product description draft**: "A starter kit for players who want to try dyeing a disc themselves
before deciding whether it's worth doing regularly — enough powder dye in a handful of colors to
attempt a first shaving-cream or lotion-resist design, no separate equipment purchase required to
get started."

**Price range**: $30–$45. *Verified via price check*: PRO Chemical's starter set and comparable
beginner dye kits found in search cluster around $25–$45 (one specific listing found at ~$44.10
for a PRO Chemical starter kit) — $30–$45 is a grounded estimate for a comparable beginner kit,
not a specific confirmed wholesale cost from either target supplier.
