## CRITICAL CORRECTION to the calendar's draft script

The concept script in `accessory-content-calendar-weeks1-3.md` (Video 1) **states the rule
incorrectly** and must not be filmed as written. It claimed:

> "Official rules say you have to mark your lie, and using your actual disc to do it means you can't
> throw from that exact spot — you're throwing from wherever the marker disc ends up instead."

**That is wrong.** Per **PDGA Rule 802.06 (Marking the Lie)** and 802.05 (Lie), verified directly at
`pdga.com/rules/official-rules-disc-golf/80206`:

- **The position of a thrown disc on the in-bounds playing surface marks the lie.** Leaving your
  thrown disc where it landed is a completely legal mark. A mini is **not required**.
- Alternatively, you may mark by **placing a mini marker disc on the playing surface, touching the
  front of the thrown disc, on the line of play.**
- A mini marker must comply with PDGA Technical Standards for mini marker discs.
- Throwing from a lie marked any other way is a **marking violation** — warning for the first
  offense.

So the honest answer to the video's own title question is **"no, you don't need one"** — which is a
*better* hook than the original, because it's contrarian, correct, and immediately useful. Publishing
a confidently wrong rules claim to an audience of rules-literate hobbyists would be the single
fastest way to lose credibility in this niche. Rewritten below.

## Final script (~32 sec, word-for-word)

> **[0:00]** Does anybody actually need one of those tiny disc golf discs? Short answer — no.
>
> **[0:04]** Here's the thing nobody tells beginners: where your thrown disc lands *already* marks
> your lie. That's in the rules. You can just leave it there, stand behind it, and throw your next
> shot. Totally legal.
>
> **[0:13]** So why does everyone carry a mini? Two real reasons.
>
> **[0:16]** One — you might want to throw *that* disc again. Drop the mini in front of it, pick your
> disc up, and now it's back in your hand instead of pinned to the ground.
>
> **[0:23]** Two — a mini is flat and small, so it's way easier to straddle and get a clean stance
> over than a full-size disc under your feet.
>
> **[0:29]** One catch: it has to touch the front of your thrown disc, on your line of play. Mark it
> wrong and that's a warning.
>
> **[0:34]** So — not required. Just genuinely convenient.

**Runtime note**: this reads at roughly 34–36 seconds at a natural pace. If you want it under 30,
cut the "Two —" beat about the flat stance; the "throw that disc again" reason is the stronger one.
