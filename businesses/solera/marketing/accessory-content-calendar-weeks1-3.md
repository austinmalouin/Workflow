# Flight Path Disc Co. — Accessory-Comparison Content Calendar (Weeks 1-3, Pre-Launch)

**Date drafted**: 2026-07-28
**Context**: There is no store yet — no domain, no products live, no supplier account active (see
`BUSINESS.md` Current State). This calendar is deliberately **platform-agnostic and store-agnostic**:
7 pieces of accessory-comparison/buying-guide content built to start growing an audience *ahead of*
the store launching, the same model the Minecraft channel uses (content first, monetization later —
see `businesses/minecraft-channel/content-calendar/launch-arc-schedule.md`). Format/structure is
modeled on RIDGEWOOD's `tiktok-scripts-week1-2.md` and `tiktok-organic-plan.md` (concept doc +
execution-ready script split), adapted for a pre-launch, no-inventory context: every video here is
either a general buying-guide (built from public product-category knowledge, not Flight Path's own
SKUs, since none exist yet) or a rules/culture explainer — nothing claims to be a review of a
Flight Path product.

**Platforms**: TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels — same vertical-video cut works on all three
with minor caption/hashtag adjustments per platform. No platform-specific account setup is assumed
done; per CLAUDE.md's standing rule, actually creating/verifying those accounts is Austin's action,
not this workspace's.

**Grounding rule** (same one RIDGEWOOD's scripts follow): no invented review counts, ratings, or
"best-selling" claims. Comparison points below are drawn from real, commonly known category
differences (towel materials, bag styles, retriever mechanisms) rather than fabricated head-to-head
test results — since no physical samples have been ordered yet, these are buying-guide/explainer
concepts, not hands-on review claims.

## Content pillar recap

Per `BUSINESS.md` Part 3: disc review/comparison content culture is real and active, but
concentrated on discs themselves (Innova Mako3 vs. Buzzz-style content). The confirmed open lane is
the **accessory layer** — bags, baskets, mini markers, towels, dye kits — where no dedicated
comparison-content brand was found in research. This calendar is the first run at owning that lane.

## Video 1 (Week 1, Day 1-2) — "Do you actually need a mini marker disc?"

**Format**: Rules-explainer, text-over-graphics or talking-head. Hooks on a genuine beginner
question rather than a product pitch.

### Concept / rough script (~30 sec)

> "If you've ever played disc golf with someone who whips out a tiny disc before they pick up
> their putter — that's a mini marker, and it's not just for show.
> Official rules say you have to mark your lie, and using your actual disc to do it means you
> can't throw from that exact spot — you're throwing from wherever the marker disc ends up instead.
> A dedicated mini marker means your good disc stays in play and you're marking the right way.
> They're small, they're cheap, and most new players don't realize they need one until their first
> real round. That's the whole reason they exist — not a gimmick, an actual rule workaround."

### On-screen text overlays

1. Opening card: **"Why does everyone carry a tiny disc?"**
2. Rule beat: **"Your lie has to be marked — using your throwing disc removes it from play"**
3. Solution beat: **"A mini marker disc solves that — cheap, small, does one job"**
4. Closing card: **"More disc golf accessory breakdowns coming — follow for the next one"**

### Shot list

- Stock/creative-commons or self-shot graphic of a disc golf basket and a lie-marking moment
  (illustration or simple animation is fine — no live footage required).
- Simple text-card cuts synced to each script beat; talking-head option if Austin wants to appear
  on camera instead of a pure text-over-graphics cut.

### Caption + hashtags

## Video 2 (Week 1, Day 4-5) — "Bag vs. bag: shoulder bag or backpack style?"

**Format**: Comparison/buyer's-guide, the calendar's first true "bag vs. bag" piece per the brief.

### Concept / rough script (~35 sec)

> "Shoulder bag or backpack — which one should you actually get?
> Shoulder bags are cheaper, lighter, and faster to grab a disc from mid-round — you sling it, you
> reach in, done. But everything you carry is on one shoulder, and most hold fewer discs.
> Backpack-style bags spread the weight across both shoulders, usually hold way more discs plus
> water and snacks, but they're bulkier and you're taking it fully off to dig through it.
> If you're playing casual rounds with a small bag of go-to discs, shoulder bag. If you're playing
> full 18 with a real rotation and you want your back to thank you later, backpack style.
> Neither one is 'wrong' — it's about how many discs you actually carry and how far you're walking."

### On-screen text overlays

1. Opening card: **"Shoulder bag or backpack — which one?"**
2. Shoulder bag beat: **"Lighter, cheaper, faster access — holds fewer discs"**
3. Backpack beat: **"More capacity, better weight distribution — bulkier, slower to dig through"**
4. Closing card: **"Depends on your rotation size and how far you walk — more comparisons coming"**

### Shot list

- Split-screen or side-by-side stock/graphic comparison of a shoulder bag silhouette vs. a backpack
  silhouette (illustration-level is fine, no physical bags needed yet).
- Text-card cuts on each comparison beat.

### Caption + hashtags

**Caption**: "shoulder bag vs backpack for disc golf — which actually fits how you play"
**Hashtags**: #discgolf #discgolfbag #discgolfgear #frisbeegolf #discgolfaccessories

### Concept / rough script (~30 sec)

> "Is a disc retriever pole actually worth carrying, or is it dead weight in your bag?

## Video 5 (Week 2, Day 6-7) — "Best disc golf towel: what actually matters"

**Format**: Buying-guide comparison — the calendar's literal "best disc golf towel" piece from the
brief.

### Concept / rough script (~30 sec)

> "Everyone says 'get a towel,' but nobody explains what actually matters when picking one.
> Material: microfiber dries fastest and doesn't hold water weight — cotton feels nicer but stays
> damp longer.
> Size: bigger isn't automatically better — too big and it doesn't dry fast wiping down a wet
> grip, too small and you're wringing it out constantly.
> Attachment: a clip that actually stays clipped beats a cute towel that falls off your bag on
> hole 3.
> That's really it — material, size, and whether the clip holds. Everything else is just color and
> branding."

### On-screen text overlays

1. Opening card: **"What actually matters in a disc golf towel"**
2. Material beat: **"Microfiber dries faster than cotton"**
3. Size beat: **"Bigger isn't automatically better"**
4. Clip beat: **"A clip that actually holds > a cute design"**
5. Closing card: **"Simple checklist, not hype — more buying guides coming"**

### Shot list

- Stock/graphic comparison of towel materials/sizes (illustration-level, no physical towel needed

**Format**: Comparison + soft teaser — the first video that gestures at the store's future

### Concept / rough script (~35 sec)

> "Dye your own disc, or just buy one already dyed — which actually makes sense for you?
> Dyeing it yourself means total creative control, and starter kits exist for under fifty bucks —
> but it's a real process: shaving cream or lotion as a resist, hours of dry time, and your first

> equipment, no learning curve, just pick a design you like.
