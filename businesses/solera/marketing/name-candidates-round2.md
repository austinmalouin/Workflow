# Name Candidates, Round 2 — Collision-Checked Before Proposing (2026-07-29)

**Why this exists**: "Flight Path Disc Co." failed its collision check (see
[name-domain-trademark-check.md](name-domain-trademark-check.md)). Austin's call: research fresh
candidates and run each through the gauntlet **before** showing him any — the opposite of what
happened last time, where a name was locked for five days and had drafts written under it before
anyone checked.

**Method**: every candidate was built from real disc golf jargon (so it reads as insider, not
invented), then checked for (a) existing disc golf businesses using the term, (b) `.com` DNS
resolution, (c) trademark signals. Candidates that failed are listed with their failure so nobody
re-proposes them later.

---

## ✅ Survivors — recommended shortlist

### 1. Chain Music Co. — **top recommendation**

- **The term is real and verified**: "chain music" is established disc golf slang for *the sound of a
  disc hitting the chains* — the most satisfying moment in the sport. Confirmed across multiple
  independent disc golf glossaries (Innova's official glossary, DiscgolfNOW's 200+ term guide,
  PowerGrip, DiscNation).
- **Niche search: clean.** No disc golf company, store, brand, or podcast found using "Chain Music."
- **Domains: `chainmusic.com` and `chainmusicco.com` — no DNS record.**
- **Why it's the pick**: it's emotional rather than technical. Every other name on this list
  describes *equipment*; this one names the *feeling players are chasing*. That's a much better fit
  for a brand whose two pillars are content and apparel, and it works on a t-shirt in a way
  "Hyzer Supply" never will. It also doesn't box the catalog in — nothing about it says
  "we only sell bags."
- **Watch**: `rattlingchains.com` is an existing disc golf blog. Different name, same imagery — worth
  knowing, not disqualifying.

### 2. Tap In Supply Co.

- **Term is real**: "tap in" = a putt so close it's a formality.
- **Niche search: clean** — nothing in disc golf found using it.
- **Domain: `tapinsupply.com` — no DNS record.**
- **Watch**: "tap in" is heavily used in ball golf and basketball, and as generic slang ("tap in to
  X"). Lower distinctiveness means a weaker trademark position and harder SEO than Chain Music.

### 3. Beat In Gear Co.

- **Term is real**: "beat in" describes a disc worn from use — and beat-in discs are *prized*, so the
  connotation is positive to anyone in the sport.
- **Niche search: clean** — nothing found.
- **Domains: `beatingear.com`, `beatingearco.com` — no DNS record.**
- **Watch — real problem**: `beatingear.com` reads as **"beating ear"** at a glance. That's a genuine
  wordmark hazard. Would need the hyphen or the "Co." (`beat-in-gear.com` /
  `beatingearco.com`), which is friction every time Austin says the URL out loud.

## ⚠️ The honest limit on this check

**Trademark status for all three survivors is UNVERIFIED, not clean.** I could not query USPTO's
`tmsearch.uspto.gov` / TESS directly in this pass — searches for specific marks returned only generic
"what is Class 28" explainer articles, and Justia's detail pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetches.
So "no trademark found" here means *no trademark surfaced in general web search*, which is weak
evidence and should not be mistaken for a clearance.

**Also unverified**: registrar-level domain availability. "No DNS record" means no live site; a domain
can be registered and parked with no DNS. Only a WHOIS check at a registrar settles it, and that's
the same screen where Austin would buy it.

**Also not checked**: social handle availability on TikTok/YouTube/Instagram — that requires being
logged in, and per the workspace rule, account/handle work is Austin's.

### Recommended sequence before spending a dollar

1. Austin picks a favorite from the three.
2. **WHOIS the `.com` at a registrar** — confirm actually available, don't buy yet.
3. **Check the handle** on TikTok (the chosen launch platform) plus YouTube and Instagram.
4. **Pay for a real trademark clearance search** on the survivor. A flat-fee attorney search is
   commonly a few hundred dollars and is worth it *before* apparel gets printed — this is the step
   the Flight Path round skipped, and it's why we're doing this twice.
5. Then buy the domain, lock the handles, and release the supplier outreach under the real name.

Steps 2–4 are Austin's actions — this workspace can't do WHOIS at a registrar, can't create handles,
and can't render a legal clearance opinion.
