# DataAnnotation Starter Assessment — Prep Guide

**Read this before you open the assessment. Not during.**

## Why this document exists

DataAnnotation pays the best of the group and has the most punishing entry:

- **10–20% acceptance rate**
- **ONE attempt — no retakes, ever, on that account**
- **AI-assisted answers = instant permanent fail.** They screen for it.
- Estimated at ~1 hour. People who actually pass report **2–3 hours.** Their own guidance says
  they grade for thoroughness and accuracy, *not* speed.

You get one shot, so we spend an hour preparing instead of finding out the hard way.

---

## Before you start — the setup checklist

- [ ] You've done **real tasks on Outlier or Alignerr first.** This is the whole reason
      DataAnnotation is sequenced fourth. Walking in cold is how the 80% fail.
- [ ] **3 uninterrupted hours blocked.** Not between shifts. Not at 11pm.
- [ ] You're **fresh** — this is a focus-heavy reading-and-writing test and fatigue shows in the
      writing quality immediately.
- [ ] Phone in another room. Notifications off.
- [ ] **No ChatGPT, no Claude, no AI tools open.** Not even "just to check grammar." Instant
      permanent fail, and they're good at detecting it.
- [ ] Water, snack, bathroom — handled before you start, because you don't want to rush the back half.

## What it actually tests

Not intelligence, not credentials. Three things:

1. **Can you read instructions carefully and follow them exactly?**
2. **Can you tell the difference between a good and a bad written response, and explain why?**
3. **Can you write clearly yourself?**

That's the whole job. The assessment is a compressed version of it.

Format is typically: short guidelines to read (5–10 pages), then sample tasks — comparing two AI
chatbot responses, rating a response against a rubric, or writing/editing text — with
multiple-choice or structured written answers.

---

## The five rules that decide pass/fail

### 1. Read every instruction twice, before answering anything

These assessments are **deliberately specific** about formatting, tone, and what makes one
response "better." Missing one stated detail is the single most common way a genuinely good answer
gets marked wrong. If the instructions say "respond in 2–3 sentences," three sentences is right and
five is wrong even if your five are better.

**Do this**: before you answer, re-read the instruction and literally check your answer against each
requirement it states.

### 2. Slow down. Seriously.

The estimate says an hour. Passers report 2–3. There is no speed bonus. There is a thoroughness
grade. If you finish in 45 minutes you have almost certainly under-answered.

### 3. Over-explain your reasoning

When a task asks *why* one response is better, the failing answer is:

> "Response A is better because it's more helpful and accurate."

The passing answer is specific, and ties back to the instructions you were given:

> "Response A is better. The user asked for steps to fix a leaking faucet, and A gives a numbered
> sequence starting with shutting off the water supply — which is both the correct first step and a
> safety issue B skips entirely. B also introduces a claim about 'most faucets being ceramic disc
> type' that isn't supported and isn't relevant to the question asked. A stays on task; B pads."

Notice what that does: names the specific difference, ties it to the user's actual request, flags a
factual problem, and identifies a pattern (padding). That's the level.

### 4. Be concrete, never generic

Banned words in your explanations unless you immediately say *why*: "helpful," "accurate,"
"better," "clear," "engaging." Those are conclusions. They want the reasoning that produced the
conclusion.

### 5. Write in complete, clean sentences

You're being evaluated as a writer, not just a rater. Capital letters, punctuation, full sentences.
Proofread before submitting each section. Typos in an assessment about writing quality are a bad look.

## Common traps

| Trap | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| A response that's longer / more detailed | Longer ≠ better. If it padded, ignored the question, or added unrequested detail, that's a *flaw*. Say so. |
| A response that sounds confident | Confidence isn't accuracy. Check whether claims are actually correct and actually relevant. |
| A response that's polite and friendly | Tone matters only if the instructions asked for it. Don't reward friendliness over correctness. |
| Both responses look fine | There is almost always a real difference. Look for: unrequested content, a skipped step, an unsupported claim, a formatting instruction ignored. |
| Both responses look bad | That's allowed and often correct. Say both fail and explain what each got wrong — don't force a winner. |
| Safety/harmful content questions | Follow the stated policy in the guidelines exactly, even if you'd personally answer differently. They're testing policy adherence. |

## Your actual advantage here

Worth saying plainly, because the acceptance rate is intimidating: **this test is well-suited to
you.** It rewards noticing detail, following stated rules precisely, and explaining reasoning in
writing. It requires no degree, no credential, no technical background, and no prior experience in
the field.

The 80% who fail mostly fail for two reasons — they rushed it, and they gave short generic answers.
Both are entirely within your control.

---

## Right before you submit

- [ ] Every answer follows the **stated format** (length, structure, whether they asked for a
      choice vs. an explanation vs. both)
- [ ] No answer is a single generic sentence
- [ ] Every "X is better" is followed by **specific** reasons tied to the task
- [ ] You proofread for typos and dropped words
- [ ] You spent **well over an hour**
- [ ] You did not use any AI tool at any point

---

## If you don't pass

It happens to 80%+ of applicants and it is not a referendum on you. The other three platforms
are still live, still paying, and have no such one-shot rule. Nothing about the plan breaks.

**Do not** make a second DataAnnotation account to retry. That's a terms violation and it gets
both accounts permanently banned — which would take the best-paying platform off the table for
good instead of just for now.
