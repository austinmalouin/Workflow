## Read this part first — the honest framing

**These platforms are legitimate.** DataAnnotation, Outlier (run by Scale AI), Alignerr (Labelbox),
Mercor, Surge, Appen, and Toloka are all real companies paying real contributors in 2026. This is
not a scam category. But it has real limits and you should plan around them, not around the
headline numbers:

| What you'll see advertised | What to actually plan for |
|---|---|
| "$50–100/hr" (Indeed's DataAnnotation listing) | **$15–20/hr effective** for general work. The high end is specialist work — PhD-level STEM, niche languages, professional coding. |
| "Start earning today" | **2–4 weeks to first dollar.** Approval is 1–14 days, then *unpaid* qualification tests, then paid work, then ~a week to first payout. |
| "Flexible full-time income" | **Income is lumpy.** Task availability fluctuates with client demand. Multiple independent reviews describe these platforms as best suited to *supplementary* income rather than a salary replacement. |

**The one thing worth saying plainly**: you picked annotation + Automation Studio, and skipped the
steady remote job. Both of those are *variable* income. If the yacht club paycheck is still coming
in, that's your floor and this plan works fine. If it isn't, there's no floor under this, and the
remote CS job option is worth reopening as a backstop — not because annotation won't work, but
because "lumpy" and "rent is due the 1st" are a bad combination without a base. Your call, said
once, not nagging.

### Scam rules — non-negotiable

- **A legitimate platform NEVER charges you to start.** Any application fee, "training package,"
  or equipment purchase = walk away.
- **Never give banking passwords during an application.** Payout setup happens *after* approval,
  inside the platform, and uses PayPal/Payoneer — not your bank login.
- **Apply only through the official URLs below.** Not through a recruiter DM, not through a link in
  a Facebook group, not through a "hiring partner."
- If anyone asks you to buy equipment and get reimbursed, that is the single most common remote-job
  scam pattern. Walk.

## ⚠️ The DataAnnotation trap — read before you touch it

DataAnnotation is the best-paying and most-recommended platform of the group. It also has the most
punishing entry:

- **Acceptance rate is 10–20%.**
- **You get ONE attempt. There are no retakes, ever, on the same account.**
- **Using ChatGPT or any AI tool to answer it is an instant, permanent fail.** They screen for it.
- Estimated at ~1 hour. People who actually pass report spending **2–3 hours**. Their own guidance
  says they review for thoroughness and accuracy, *not* speed.

**Therefore: DataAnnotation is not the first application you submit. It's the fourth.** Build reps
on the lower-stakes platforms first, learn what these tasks actually feel like, then take
DataAnnotation's one shot when you know what you're doing.

## The application order (do it in this sequence)

### Week 1, Day 1 — apply to these three today

These have retakes, rolling assessments, or lower stakes. Applying costs you an hour total and
starts the approval clock, which is the slowest part of the whole process.

| # | Platform | Official URL | What it pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outlier** (Scale AI) | `outlier.ai` | $15–50+/hr depending on track | Biggest task volume of the group. General writing/evaluation tracks are open to non-specialists. Weekly payouts. |
| 2 | **Alignerr** (Labelbox) | `alignerr.com` | Varies by project | Smaller, but real. Worth having as a second source when Outlier task volume dips. |
| 3 | **Mercor** | `mercor.com` | Varies, often interview-based | Slightly different model — more matching-to-projects than open task queues. Free to apply. |

**Why these first**: they diversify your task supply. The #1 complaint across every review is that
task availability is inconsistent on any single platform. Being on three means a dry week on one
doesn't zero you out.

### Week 1, Day 3–5 — prepare, then take the DataAnnotation assessment

Only after you've seen what the other platforms' work actually looks like.

| Platform | Official URL | Pay | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DataAnnotation** | `dataannotation.tech` | $15–20/hr realistic (advertised much higher) | 10–20% acceptance, **one shot, no retakes** |

**How to pass it** — from people who did:

1. **Read every instruction twice before answering anything.** The assessments are deliberately
   specific about formatting, tone, and what makes one response "better" than another. Missing one
   stated detail is the single most common way a good answer gets marked wrong.
2. **Budget 2–3 hours and do it in one sitting when you're fresh.** Not at 11pm. Not between shifts.
   This is the one-shot test — treat it like it matters, because it does.
3. **Do NOT use AI to help.** Instant permanent fail. They screen for it specifically.
4. **Write more than feels necessary.** They're grading thoroughness. Where a task asks you to
   explain *why* one AI response is better, give the actual reasoning — specific, concrete, tied to
   the instructions you were given. Short answers read as low-effort.
5. **Your actual advantage here**: this work is fundamentally *evaluating whether written responses
   are good, clear, and follow instructions.* You're articulate and you notice detail. That is
   genuinely the skill being tested — more than any credential.

## What the work actually is

So there's no surprise on day one. You'll be doing some mix of:

- **Comparing two AI chatbot responses** and explaining which is better and why
- **Rating a single response** against a rubric (helpfulness, accuracy, tone, safety)
- **Rewriting/editing AI output** to be correct or better written
- **Writing prompts** designed to test a model
- Occasionally: fact-checking, categorizing, transcription-adjacent work

It's reading and writing, on your own schedule, alone. No calls, no shifts, no customers. That's
the point.

## Realistic income math

Don't build a budget on the advertised numbers. Build it on this:

| Scenario | Hours/week | Effective rate | Weekly | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (one platform, patchy tasks) | 15 | $16 | $240 | ~$1,040 |
| Realistic (2–3 platforms, steady) | 25 | $18 | $450 | ~$1,950 |
| Good week (high task availability) | 35 | $20 | $700 | ~$3,030 |

**The honest caveat**: the "realistic" row assumes task availability holds, which it does not always
do. Some weeks are dry. This is why being on 3+ platforms matters, and why a floor from other
income matters.

Also: this is **1099 contractor income.** No taxes withheld. Set aside roughly 25–30% for taxes or
you'll owe a nasty bill. Worth talking to a CPA once income is real — same one who can answer the
sole-prop-vs-LLC question already sitting in the disc golf notes.
