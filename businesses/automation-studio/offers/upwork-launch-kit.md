# Upwork Launch Kit — Automation Studio

**Built 2026-07-26.** Austin's route to client #1 with zero network, no reviews, and a stated
dislike of selling. Everything here is copy-paste ready.

**Core principle:** Upwork is *written* sales, not spoken. Clients post a job with a budget and
come to you. Austin's job is to click; the writing is the agent's job.

## The "click a few buttons" workflow

Austin is not writing proposals. This is the loop:

1. **Austin finds a job** on Upwork that looks reasonable (or saves the link).
2. **He pastes the job posting** into the Automation Studio chat — the whole thing, raw.
3. **The agent writes the custom proposal** in under a minute, tuned to that specific posting
   using the frameworks above.
4. **Austin pastes it in and clicks Submit.**

That's the whole job. He never writes a proposal from scratch.

### Two rules that make this work

**Speed beats polish.** Upwork rewards early applicants — replies drop sharply after the first
hour, and most posts collect 20–50 proposals within a day. A good proposal sent in 20 minutes
beats a perfect one sent in six hours. Austin should check Upwork twice a day and send jobs over
immediately.

**Target clients who are new to Upwork.** Clients with no hire history are significantly more
open to freelancers with no reviews — they don't yet know to filter for a 100% Job Success
Score. Filter for these deliberately while building the first three reviews.

## What Austin actually has to do himself

Being straight about this — these cannot be delegated:

1. **Create the Upwork account and verify ID.** Account creation and identity verification are
   his, always. (~20 min)
2. **Paste in the profile copy above.** (~10 min)
3. **Add the portfolio piece** with Bridge screenshots. (~20 min)
4. **Buy Connects** — Upwork charges for proposal credits. Budget ~$10–20/month starting out.
5. **Send the proposals.** The agent writes them; Austin clicks Submit.
6. **Talk to the client once they reply.** Usually chat/messages, occasionally a call. This is
   the only unavoidable human part, and it's a conversation about their problem — not a hard sell.

Total setup: roughly one hour, once.

### What this confirms

1. **$55/hr is correctly placed — arguably conservative.** Real postings sit at $50–75 and
   $70–90 for exactly this work. Austin is bidding *under* market to win early reviews, which is
   the right trade, not a discount he's stuck with.

2. **The Kindermusik posting is the ideal-client template.** "Not a build-me-a-Zap project…
   we're equally interested in your ability to think through business systems." That is Austin's
   positioning verbatim. Jobs phrased this way should be top priority — they're less
   price-competitive and reward the operator story.

3. **Trades businesses are already hiring on Upwork.** The HouseCall Pro job is field-service
   software (HVAC/plumbing/contractors). This means the local-trades thesis and the Upwork
   channel are the *same market*, not two separate plans — experience won on Upwork transfers
   directly to local outreach later.

### The single most useful filter — use this every time

Upwork's sidebar has a **Client history** filter. On this search:

- **No hires: 49 jobs**
- 1–9 hires: 47 jobs

Roughly **half of all listings come from clients who have never hired on Upwork.** These clients
haven't yet learned to filter for a high Job Success Score, which makes them dramatically more
winnable for someone with zero reviews.

**Standing instruction: filter to "No hires" + "1 to 9 hires" until Austin has 3 reviews.**

Also useful from the same sidebar: fixed-price work skews small ($100–500: 16 jobs; $500–1K: 8),
and 52 jobs are longer than 6 months with 24 marked contract-to-hire — meaning a small first win
can turn into ongoing work.

## Honest expectations

- **First job takes 10–40 proposals.** That is normal, not failure. Reviews are the bottleneck,
  and the only way through it is volume early.
- **First 2–3 jobs are about reviews, not money.** Take slightly small or slightly boring work if
  it ends in five stars. The rate climbs fast afterward.
- **Upwork takes a cut** (roughly 10%) — factor it into the rate.
- **Global price competition is real.** Austin will lose bids to $20/hr offshore freelancers and
  should let those go. He wins on being US-based, responsive, plain-spoken, and genuinely
  operating what he sells — never on price.
- **Raw AI-written proposals get filtered.** This is why the workflow is per-job custom writing
  rather than a mass-send template. It is the difference between this working and not.
