# Automation Studio — AI automation consulting for local trades

**Agent**: `automation-agent` | **Skill**: (reuses research + drafting patterns) | **Status**: onboarding (founded 2026-07-26)

## What this is

Austin's first **service business** — selling AI automation setup to local trade/service
businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping, cleaning, auto, contractors) in North
Carolina.

This venture exists to solve a specific gap in the portfolio: **every other venture Austin owns
is an asset play.** Etsy, RIDGEWOOD, Flight Path, the Minecraft channel, and CraftHaven all
compound over months and all sit at $0 today. None of them produce income this month. This one
is the cash engine — it trades hours for money at a high rate while the assets mature.

**Target: $100+/hr effective, first paid client within 30 days of starting outreach.**

## Why this venture, and why now (research-grounded 2026-07-26)

Austin already built a multi-agent business workspace with specialized agents, skills,
cross-business orchestration, and a custom control app (the Bridge). That is not a hobby project
— it is a **demonstrable portfolio piece and a genuinely scarce skill.** The gap between "I use
ChatGPT sometimes" and what he has actually built is enormous, and small businesses pay to close
it.

Market reality, from live research (sources in the 2026-07-26 research block below):

| Tier | Rate |
|---|---|
| Offshore freelancers (price competition) | $25–75/hr |
| **US solo experts** | **$80–200/hr** |
| Boutique consultancies | $150–300/hr |
| Big 4 | $300–600/hr |

**82% of small-business employers have already invested in AI tools.** Demand is not the
problem — implementation capacity is. Highest-ROI categories buyers are actually paying for:
customer support deflection, email/follow-up automation, and connecting existing apps.

## Niche decision — local trades & service businesses (2026-07-26)

**Decided: local trades.** Austin was genuinely unsure about his network ("not really sure"),
which is normal and does **not** block this — local trades is the right beginner niche
regardless of who he already knows, for reasons that are structural rather than personal:

1. **The owner is the decision-maker.** One conversation, no procurement, no committee, no
   security review. This is the single biggest reason to avoid corporate/professional-office
   niches early.
2. **The pain is obvious, expensive, and countable.** A missed call at an HVAC company is a
   $500–5,000 job walking away. That makes ROI trivial to demonstrate — no abstract
   "productivity gains" hand-waving required.
3. **Local beats offshore on trust.** Austin cannot compete with $25/hr offshore labor on price,
   but he can show up in person in North Carolina. For a trades owner, that is decisive.
4. **Low AI adoption, high willingness to pay.** These businesses are behind on tooling but
   already spend real money on lead generation and answering services.
5. **Recurring pain = recurring revenue.** Once a workflow runs their business, someone has to
   maintain it.

**Validation plan before heavy investment** (because the network question is unresolved): list
20 local businesses Austin has personally interacted with — his own mechanic, barber, gym,
landscaper, whoever did work on his house — and have 10 *conversations, not pitches*, asking
only "what eats your time every week?" If missed calls / quote follow-up / scheduling come up
repeatedly, the niche is confirmed and the offer below is already built for it. If something
else dominates, re-scope the offer around that instead. This costs a week and zero dollars.

## The offer ladder (pricing set 2026-07-26)

Hourly billing is deliberately **not** the model — it caps income and punishes Austin for being
fast with AI. Packaged pricing instead:

**Rung 1 — The paid audit: $500.**
90-minute discovery call + a short written report naming the 2–3 highest-impact automations that
business could build, with estimated hours/dollars saved. ~3 hours of work → **~$165/hr, on the
very first sale, with no portfolio required.** This is the wedge: it converts "I'm new at this"
into "I found you $2,000/month of wasted time," which is a far easier sale than a cold retainer.

**Rung 2 — Implementation: $1,500–3,000 per workflow.**
The audit already diagnosed the problem and quoted the fix, so this is an obvious next step
rather than a cold ask.

**Rung 3 — Maintenance retainer: $200–500/mo.**
Someone has to keep it running, monitor failures, and adjust it.

### Honest pricing caveat — do not quote the big numbers to small trades

Published research cites SMB retainers of $1,000–3,500/mo and medians of $2,800–7,000/mo, but
**that "SMB" usually means 20–200 employees.** A three-person landscaping company will not pay
$3,000/month, and quoting that will kill the deal and the relationship. The pricing above is
scoped deliberately to the small end of local trades. The larger numbers only apply to
multi-workflow engagements with businesses that have real headcount — which is a legitimate
place to grow into later, not where to start.

## The flagship automation: missed-call text-back

The single easiest sale in this niche, and the recommended lead offer:

When a call to the business goes unanswered, an automated text fires back within seconds —
"Sorry we missed you, this is [Business]. What do you need help with?" — and captures the lead
into a simple pipeline instead of losing it to whoever the customer calls next.

Why this specific one leads: the ROI math is self-evident to the owner (one saved job pays for
the entire build), it's fast to implement, it requires no change to how they already work, and
it produces a visible win within days that justifies the next project.

## Honest risks

1. **Sales is the bottleneck, not delivery.** Austin can already build this. Getting someone to
   say yes is the actual skill being bought with month one. This is the most likely failure point.
2. **Cannot compete on price.** Offshore is $25–75/hr. Win on niche knowledge, local presence,
   and trust — never on being cheapest.
3. **Trades owners are hard to reach.** They're on job sites, not answering email. Phone and
   in-person beat cold email badly in this niche.
4. **Scope creep on fixed-price work** destroys effective hourly rate. Every engagement needs a
   written scope and a change-order rule.
5. **This trades hours for money.** It funds the asset ventures; it does not replace them.
