# Outreach playbook — validation conversations → paid audit

**Drafted 2026-07-26. Nothing here has been sent.** Austin sends everything himself.

---

## Phase 0 — Build the list (30 minutes, do this first)

Write down **20 local businesses you have personally interacted with.** Not strangers — people
who already know your face or your name. Prompts to jog it loose:

- Who fixed your car, your HVAC, your plumbing, your roof?
- Your gym, barber, tattoo artist, dentist, vet?
- Who mows/landscapes anything you or your family owns?
- Any business your friends or family own or work at?
- Anyone you've hired for anything in the last 3 years?

**Rule: warm beats cold by an enormous margin in this niche.** The research is unanimous that
your first client comes from your network or one degree out. Do not start with cold outreach.

Save the list as `outreach/target-list.md` with: business name, contact, how you know them,
what you'd guess their biggest time-waster is.

## Phase 1 — Validation conversations (week 1)

**Goal: 10 conversations. You are NOT selling.** You are confirming that the pain you think
exists actually exists, in their words, so your offer uses their language instead of yours.

This is also the lowest-pressure way to open a sales relationship — and several of these will
convert on their own without you ever pitching.

### The ask (text or in person)

> Hey [Name] — random question. I'm doing some work around automating the boring admin stuff for
> local businesses, and I'm trying to understand what actually eats people's time before I build
> anything. Could I buy you a coffee / grab 15 min sometime and just ask you about how you handle
> calls and scheduling? Not selling you anything, genuinely just research.

### What to ask (let them talk, take notes, do not pitch)

1. Walk me through what happens when someone calls you and you're on a job.
2. How do you keep track of quotes you've sent that haven't come back yet?
3. What's the thing you keep meaning to get to but never do?
4. How do you ask for reviews?
5. If you could delete one recurring task from your week, what is it?
6. What are you using right now — anything? Spreadsheets, notebook, a CRM?

### What you're listening for

| If they say | That's the opening for |
|---|---|
| "I miss calls all the time when I'm on a job" | **Missed-call text-back** (lead with this) |
| "I send quotes and never hear back" | Quote follow-up sequence |
| "I never remember to ask for reviews" | Review request automation |
| "Scheduling is a nightmare / phone tag" | Booking + reminder automation |
| "I've got it all in my head / a notebook" | Simple lead intake + pipeline |

**Stop after 10.** If missed calls, quote follow-up, or scheduling came up 5+ times, the niche is
confirmed — proceed to Phase 2. If something completely different dominated, tell the automation
agent what you heard and the offer gets re-scoped around that instead.

## Phase 2 — Convert a conversation into a paid audit

Some of these people will ask "so what would you actually do?" during the validation call. That's
your opening. **Do not improvise a free solution on the spot** — that's how you end up doing $2,000
of thinking for free.

### The pivot line

> Honestly, based on what you just told me, I think there's a few hours a week sitting right
> there. What I do is a paid audit — I spend 90 minutes going through your actual setup, then
> write you up the two or three highest-impact things to automate, what each one would save you,
> and what it'd cost to build. That's $500, and it's yours whether or not you have me build any
> of it. If you do want me to build it, that $500 comes off the build.

### Why this works

- **$500 is a small enough number** for a trades owner to decide alone, on the spot.
- **"Yours whether or not you have me build it"** removes the fear of being trapped in a sales trap.
- **Credit it toward the build** makes saying yes to the next rung nearly automatic.
- You get **paid to do your sales call** — which is what makes this ~$165/hr from day one.

### If they hesitate on $500

Do **not** discount to $250. Instead, narrow the scope:

> Totally fair. Want me to just look at the missed-call thing specifically? That's a smaller
> piece — $250, and I'll tell you exactly what it'd take.

Discounting the same offer teaches them your prices are soft. Shrinking the offer doesn't.

## Phase 3 — The missed-call text-back pitch (your flagship)

Lead with this one. It's the easiest sale in the trades because the owner does the ROI math
himself before you finish the sentence.

### The pitch, in their language

> When someone calls you and you're up on a roof, what happens right now? They hang up and call
> the next guy on Google, right? So what I set up is: the second a call goes unanswered, they get
> a text back — "Sorry we missed you, this is [Business], what do you need?" — and it lands in one
> place so you can answer it that evening instead of losing it. Most guys I talk to are missing
> somewhere between five and fifteen calls a week. What's one job worth to you?

Then stop talking. Let them do the math out loud. They will.

### Pricing this specific build

- Audit: **$500** (credited toward the build)
- Missed-call text-back build: **$1,500**
- Ongoing monitoring/adjustments: **$200/mo** (optional, offer after it's proven working)

If one saved job is worth $500–5,000 to them, a $1,500 build that catches 5+ missed calls a week
pays for itself almost immediately. **Say that math out loud** — don't assume they've done it.

## Phase 4 — Follow-up (where most of the money actually is)

Most deals in this niche close on contact 3–5, not contact 1. Trades owners are not ignoring
you, they are on a job site.

**Cadence after a good conversation with no decision:**

- **Day 3** — text: *"Hey [Name], been thinking about the missed-call thing for you. Still happy to do that audit whenever — no rush."*
- **Day 10** — send something useful with no ask: an article, or a note about what another local business did.
- **Day 24** — text: *"Checking in one more time — want me to take a look at that? If it's not a priority right now that's totally fine, I'll leave you alone."*

That last line closes more deals than any pitch, because it gives them permission to say no —
which is exactly why many of them finally say yes.

**Phone and in-person beat email badly in this niche.** Do not build an email-first sequence.

---

## Hard rules

1. **Never quote the big agency numbers** ($3k/mo retainers) to a three-person trades business.
   It kills the deal and the relationship. See the pricing caveat in `../BUSINESS.md`.
2. **Every fixed-price job needs a written scope** and a change-order rule, or scope creep will
   destroy your effective hourly rate.
3. **Don't build anything before the audit is paid for.** The audit *is* the product at rung 1.
4. **Don't promise a specific dollar outcome.** "Most businesses in your spot are missing 5–15
   calls a week" is honest. "This will make you $4,000/month" is not, and it's the kind of claim
   that turns a happy client into an angry one.
