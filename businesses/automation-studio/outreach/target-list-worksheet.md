# The 20-Business Target List — Worksheet

**This is the thing blocking Automation Studio.** Not the offer (built), not the pricing (set), not
the scripts (drafted). Just this list. It takes about 30 minutes and costs nothing.

---

## Why it's *your* list and not a list I built for you

I looked up Wilmington trades businesses. What came back was Blaze Air (40+ years, HVAC + plumbing
+ electrical), Odyssey Mechanical, O'Brien Service Company — **all large, established operations.**

Those are the wrong first target, for two reasons:

1. **They're too big.** `BUSINESS.md` already warns against quoting SMB numbers to small trades — but
   the inverse is also true: a 40-year-old multi-service company likely has an office manager, an
   answering service, and existing systems. Your missed-call pitch lands weakest there.
2. **They're cold.** You'd be a stranger with no portfolio, cold-calling a business that gets pitched
   constantly.

**Warm beats cold enormously for a first client, and small beats big.** The businesses where you've
been a customer are the ones where you can open with "hey, you did my brakes in March" instead of
"hi, I'd like to talk about AI." That's a completely different conversation.

So: this is a memory-jogger, not a directory.

## Part 2 — Filling the gap (only if you're short of 20)

If the warm list comes up short, top up with genuinely **small** local operations — not the big
established firms.

**How to find the right size on Angi or Yelp** (`angi.com`, `yelp.com`, search Wilmington NC):

Look for these signals of a small owner-operated business:
- Owner's name in the business name ("Dave's Plumbing," not "Coastal Mechanical Group")
- Under ~50 reviews
- No slick corporate website, or no website at all
- The phone number goes to a cell, not a menu tree
- Listed hours like "7am–5pm" not "24/7 dispatch"

**Avoid**: anything advertising 24/7 dispatch, multiple service lines, decades in business, or a
fleet. Those have systems already and you're the tenth pitch this month.

## Part 3 — What you actually say

**This is not a pitch. It's 10 conversations.** Per `BUSINESS.md`, the goal is to find out whether
missed calls / quote follow-up / scheduling really are the pain — before you build an offer around
assuming they are.

The opener, for someone who knows you:

> "Hey — random question, not selling anything. I'm looking at starting something helping small
> businesses around here automate the annoying repetitive stuff. What's the thing that eats your
> time every week that you wish just handled itself?"

Then **shut up and listen.** Don't pitch. Don't mention AI unless they do. You're collecting data.

**Listen for**: missed calls, chasing quotes, scheduling back-and-forth, invoice follow-up, repeating
the same answers to customers.

- If those come up repeatedly → the niche is confirmed and your offer already fits it.
- If something else dominates → **that's the real finding**, and the offer gets rescoped around it.
  That's a success, not a failure.

## The honest expectation

Per `BUSINESS.md`: **month one is mostly unpaid outreach, and that's expected, not a sign it isn't
working.** Sales is the bottleneck here, not delivery — you can already build this stuff. The skill
month one buys is getting someone to say yes.

Also worth being straight about: **this track is slower than the annotation work.** Realistically
4–12 weeks to a first paid audit. It's the higher ceiling and it's genuinely yours — but the
annotation income is what pays rent while this ripens. Don't let a slow week here feel like failure;
they're on different clocks by design.
